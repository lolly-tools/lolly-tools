How to read this chart

The filled area is every colour that exists at this slice through OKLCH space. The solid white line is where sRGB ends — the gamut every screen, print pipeline and older browser can be trusted with. The dashed line is Display-P3. Anything past a line is washed back because this sheet is sRGB and physically cannot show it to you; the line is the information, not the colour beyond it.

The boundary moves with hue, and that is the whole point: a yellow reaches roughly twice the chroma of a blue. It is why an evenly-stepped ramp goes lopsided across hues, and why a colour can look duller once exported than it did in the picker.

Chroma headroom is the actionable number — how much more chroma this hue and lightness could take before sRGB runs out. A negative value means the colour is already outside and is being mapped down every time it renders.
